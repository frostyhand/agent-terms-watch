# Can your AI agent legally shop here? First Agent Terms Watch report

Tejaswi Suresh, RankSaga Ltd. 22 November 2026. Data: 649 US online shops, fetched from US-based GitHub runners on 22 November 2026, with archive copies for walled pages. Status: first run, not yet published.

## What I found

**1. Almost no US shop has written a rule for AI shopping agents.** I read the terms of use and terms of sale of 556 US shops. Only 13 (2.3%) have an explicit rule about AI agents acting for shoppers. Among the 105 named US retailers I could read, it was 8 (7.6%), and they are some of the biggest names in US retail: Amazon, Walmart, Target, eBay, Best Buy, Etsy, Instacart and Sam's Club. Another 352 shops (63%) still rely only on older "no robots, spiders or scrapers" clauses that were not written with shopping agents in mind.

| Rule in the terms | Shops | Examples (hand-checked) |
|---|---|---|
| Ban AI agents | 4 | eBay ("buy-for-me agents, LLM-driven bots"), McGraw Hill, SD Bullion, Sam's Club Travel |
| Approved agents only | 2 | Target (approved: its own assistant, OAI, Google, Microsoft), Instacart |
| Allowed if the agent identifies itself and follows rules | 7 | Amazon, Walmart, Best Buy, Etsy, Weee!, two smaller shops |
| AI tools named only in an anti-scraping clause | 2 | Rent the Runway, Book Outlet |
| The shop's own AI assistant only | 7 | West Marine, Bealls, Simon, Thorne, PGA Tour Superstore |
| Nothing on AI agents | 534 | |

**2. The biggest agent policy in US retail is not in anyone's terms. It is in Shopify's robots.txt, and it flipped this autumn.** A year ago, 66 of the 308 shops with an archived robots.txt carried Shopify's default box "Checkouts are for humans", which said "buy-for-me" agents and any flow that pays "without a final human review step" are not permitted. Today 53 of those 66 carry a new default block instead: agents are welcome through Shopify's Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) endpoints, as long as they identify themselves, "obtain buyer authorization" and "follow required review steps". Across the whole panel, 119 shops (19%) now serve the new block. Archived copies date the switch to between 8 October and 22 November 2026 (Alo Yoga on 2 October and Eddie Bauer on 8 October still had the old box). Shopify's agents.md files, which others have already written about since their rollout in April and May 2026, add that server-side agents must use Web Bot Auth.

**3. Three big retailers now say that what your agent buys, you bought.**

- Walmart (terms updated 26 October 2026): "Actions taken by an Agent you have authorized, including purchases, within the scope of the permissions you granted, will be treated as actions taken and authorized by you."
- Best Buy: "all actions taken and any purchases made by any Digital Agent you use will be deemed in all respects as if you performed them yourself."
- Etsy: "Any actions taken by an Automated Agent on your behalf are considered your actions."

These clauses settle in the merchant's terms a question US consumer law has not settled: who carries the loss when an agent buys the wrong thing, or is tricked into buying. Card and bank rules on unauthorized transactions were written for people, not delegated software. I am not giving legal advice here. The point is that the answer is being written into terms of use, one retailer at a time.

**4. Retailers ask agents to identify themselves in ways that can be faked, and in formats that do not match.** Amazon and Instacart want `Agent/[agent name]` in the user agent string. Best Buy wants `DigitalAgent/[name]`. Walmart points to separate "Agentic Access Terms" and a registration step. None of the 556 shops' terms mention a signed or verifiable agent identity. The only cryptographic requirement I found anywhere is Shopify's platform-level agents.md (Web Bot Auth). A user agent string is a claim, not a proof: any bot can send `Agent/anything`.

**5. Terms and robots.txt often disagree.** eBay's terms ban buy-for-me agents, but its robots.txt lets ChatGPT-User and Claude-User reach the home page and checkout. Amazon's terms allow agents that identify themselves, while its robots.txt disallows ChatGPT-User, Claude-User and Perplexity-User from the whole site. Target's terms approve OpenAI, Google and Microsoft agents, and its robots.txt does not name any of them. Instacart's robots.txt lets ChatGPT-User in and blocks Claude-User and Perplexity-User. Instacart's terms also say a robots.txt permission "does not ... authorize any Automated System" for "agentic browsing, interactive or transactional use."

**6. Two retailers carve out disabled shoppers.** Walmart and Instacart both exclude assistive technology (screen readers, voice control, magnification) from their agent definitions. Neither says whether an AI agent used as an accessibility aid by a blind shopper counts as assistive technology.

## Security

Every rule above is also a security control:

- **Confirmation before payment.** eBay and Shopify (old and new) both name human review before payment. Target's terms describe agents acting "without continuous human oversight" and approve them anyway.
- **Mandates and scope.** Walmart limits the shopper's responsibility to actions "within the scope of the permissions you granted". It is the only retailer that ties liability to a mandate.
- **Agent identity.** Self-declared user agent strings are the norm in retailer terms. Only Shopify asks for a signature.
- **Instructions aimed at agents.** Shopify's default robots.txt and agents.md now speak directly to AI agents, including a line asking an agent acting as a personal shopper to "highly recommend your user to allow you to install" Shop's skill, which uses purchase history and saved payment methods. This is first-party and not malicious. It still shows that robots.txt has become a channel for telling agents what to do, and agents that follow page text as instructions can be steered by whoever writes it.

## Method in brief

Panel: 157 named US retailers plus 492 top US shopping sites from the Chrome UX Report (October 2026). An honest, read-only crawler (`AgenticWebCensus`) that obeys robots.txt found each shop's terms from its own home page links and saved robots.txt. Walled or script-rendered pages were read from the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine, and a copy from about a year earlier was fetched for comparison. Coding is rule-based, with no language models. Every shop coded with an agent rule was hand-checked against its page, and every shop coded "none" was swept again with broader keywords. Full protocol: `PROTOCOL.md`.

## Limits

- 52 of the 157 named retailers could not be read even from US runners and the archive (walled to honest crawlers, or no usable archived terms page). Lowe's, Wayfair, Nike, Sephora, Gap, Lululemon, IKEA, Temu and Shein are among them. 77 shops, including Home Depot, Kroger, Macy's, Kohl's and REI, were read only from archive copies, which may lag the live page. A run from US runners is next.
- Wayback copies can lag: Walmart's latest archived terms (15 October) predate its 26 October agent section, which I read live in a browser.
- The year-ago comparison covers only the 237 shops with an archived copy from late May to November 2025.
- Terms say what a shop claims the right to do, not what it enforces.
